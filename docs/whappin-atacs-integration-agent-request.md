# Agent Request: Contemplate a Flagship ATACS Integration for Whappin

## Purpose of This Request

Review the organization **ATACS — Asociación Tribal Afrodescendiente del Caribe Sur** and determine how Whappin should represent it with the same level of care, depth, usefulness, and custom integration thinking used for the Caribbean Guard demonstration.

This is **not** a request to copy the Caribbean Guard page mechanically.

Caribbean Guard is an emergency-response and public-safety organization. ATACS is a community, cultural, historical, advocacy, and territorial-rights organization. The interaction model, calls to action, page architecture, safeguards, and supporting features must reflect those differences.

The objective is to determine how Whappin can give ATACS a flagship-quality public interface rather than a conventional directory listing.

Reference implementation:

- Caribbean Guard flagship page: https://www.whappin.com/businesses/caribbean-guard.html
- ATACS website: https://atacspv.org/
- Whappin: https://www.whappin.com/

---

# Core Assignment

Study the existing Caribbean Guard implementation and the ATACS research package below.

Then:

1. Analyze the interaction, layout, information hierarchy, visual treatment, source handling, disclosures, and specialized functionality used on the Caribbean Guard page.
2. Identify which principles should carry over to ATACS.
3. Identify which Caribbean Guard mechanics must **not** carry over because they are inappropriate for a cultural and community organization.
4. Propose a flagship ATACS integration designed specifically for its mission, public role, projects, historical material, events, leadership, advocacy work, and community participation.
5. Determine what can be built from public information now.
6. Identify which facts, geographic mappings, project statuses, leadership details, permissions, and organizational claims require direct confirmation from ATACS.
7. Return a concrete implementation proposal, not merely a conceptual discussion.
8. Challenge the assumptions in this document where the available evidence or current Whappin architecture suggests a better approach.

The final proposal should be useful to both a stakeholder reviewing the concept and an implementation agent building it.

# Working Principle

The Caribbean Guard page succeeds because it is not merely a prettier directory card.

It gives the organization a useful public interface with:

- a strong identity,
- clear immediate actions,
- structured programs and services,
- geographic relevance,
- leadership and organizational context,
- purpose-built functionality,
- impact information,
- source transparency,
- disclosures,

ATACS deserves the same **level of care**, but not the same literal feature set.

The ATACS page should be conceived as a public interface for:

- Afro-Caribbean cultural preservation,
- historical education,
- ancestral geography,
- community participation,
- cultural programming,
- regional organization,
- advocacy,
- public documents,
- and events.

The goal is not to create institutional propaganda. The goal is to create a respectful, useful, well-sourced, carefully attributed community profile.

ATACS describes itself as an initiative created by people originating from Costa Rica’s South Caribbean.

Its stated purpose is to unite, defend, and represent the legal, cultural, economic, and social rights of Afro-descendant people from the communities of the region.

Its public work appears to sit at the intersection of:

- cultural preservation,
- Afro-Caribbean identity,
- historical recovery,
- ancestral place names,

# Published Vision and Advocacy Priorities

ATACS’s site identifies several major priorities.

## 1. Recognition of Cocklass

ATACS refers to territory presently associated with Kéköldi as land its ancestors called **Cocklass**.

It seeks legal recognition and economic rights for Afro-descendant communities in relation to that territory.

This is a sensitive historical, legal, political, and territorial position.

Whappin must present it as:

- “ATACS states…”
- “According to the organization…”
- “ATACS advocates for…”

Do not present the claim as independently adjudicated fact.

## 2. Maritime-Terrestrial-Zone Rights

ATACS argues that implementation of Costa Rica’s Maritime-Terrestrial Zone framework has affected areas of historically Afro-Caribbean territory.

It seeks legal measures intended to recognize or restore rights of originating Afro-descendant communities.

Again, use attributed language.

## 3. Recovery of History and Traditions

ATACS seeks to recover and publicize:

- historical values transmitted by ancestors,
- community traditions,
- distinctive cultural characteristics,
- regional memory,
- and recognition of the community as a Costa Rican tribal culture with deep historical roots.

## 4. Regional Unity

ATACS seeks to create or strengthen an organization capable of unifying Afro-descendant communities throughout the South Caribbean.

## 5. August Heritage Activities

ATACS promotes annual recognition of Afro-descendant contributions to:

- sports,
- art,
- politics,
- education,
- and national life.

Its site connects this work to Costa Rican Law No. 9526, which recognizes August as the historical month of Afro-descendancy.

## 6. Legal Representation

ATACS seeks ongoing legal representation to defend community integrity and socioeconomic rights.

## 7. Community Support

The organization broadly states that it exists to support Afro-descendant society throughout the South Caribbean.

# Organizational History

ATACS traces its roots to **Fuerza Caribe**, an organized group formed around 2015.

Public cultural records alternatively describe the organization as having been created in 2017.

These dates may represent:

- the informal predecessor beginning in 2015,
- followed by formal organization or registration in 2017.

Do not collapse the dates into one unsupported statement.

The site says Fuerza Caribe included people connected to:

- Cámara de Turismo y Comercio del Caribe Sur,
- Asociación de Desarrollo Integral de Puerto Viejo,
- and a group of Afro-descendant women.

ATACS later formalized as an association.

Its history includes efforts to strengthen connections between South Caribbean communities and national institutions.

The site also references support for the appointment of Enrique Joseph as Presidential Commissioner for Afro-descendant Affairs and historical or cultural coordination with the vice-presidential office.

The exact chronology and legal-registration milestones should be confirmed before publication.

# Representation and Legitimacy Dispute

A serious implementation must not omit that ATACS’s representative legitimacy has been contested by at least some community members.

A 2024 public statement attributed to members of the Afro-Costa Rican tribal population of Cahuita criticized the Foro del Pueblo Tribal Afrocostarricense and ATACS’s role in it.

The statement alleged that some representatives were self-appointed rather than democratically chosen and argued that ATACS, as a private organization, could not automatically speak for all Afro-descendant people of Cahuita.

Source document:

https://surcosdigital.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/COMUNICADO-PUBLICO-POBLACION-AFROCOSTARRICENSE-DE-CAHUITA-COSTA-RICA.pdf

This is:

- a public allegation,
- a political position,
- and evidence of internal disagreement.

It is **not** a final legal adjudication proving misconduct.

Whappin should therefore avoid language such as:

- “the official representative of all Afro-descendant people,”
- “the sole voice of the community,”
- or any equivalent exclusive claim.

The page does not necessarily need to foreground this dispute as a controversy section unless editorially justified.

However, its existence should shape the wording and sourcing throughout the profile.

# Editorial Guardrails

Whappin should not claim that ATACS:

- represents every Afro-descendant resident,
- is the sole or exclusive representative body,
- has uncontested authority over Afro-Caribbean heritage,
- legally owns or controls disputed territory,
- currently operates every project mentioned on its website,
- organizes every event with which it is associated,
- maintains the same board currently displayed online,
- or is formally named “ATAC” if its preferred acronym is ATACS.

Whappin should not:

- flatten complex advocacy into tourism copy,
- commercialize community history,
- turn territorial claims into map facts without attribution,
- use ceremonial or heritage imagery without permission,
- scrape personal biographies or photographs without a lawful and respectful basis,
- collect membership or sensitive identity information without an approved data process,
- or use community-submitted historical content without moderation and attribution.
